Dataset

MNIST (Yann LeCun, Corinna Cortes and Christopher Burges, 1998) has 70,000 handwritten digits taken from NIST's databases: 60,000 for training and 10,000 for testing, written by US Census Bureau employees and high-school students. The training and test sets come from different writers. Each digit was scaled to fit a 20×20 box, anti-aliased to grayscale, and placed in a 28×28 image with its centre of mass in the middle.

train.py downloads the four files from the Google Cloud mirror (storage.googleapis.com/cvdf-datasets/mnist/), because the original site at yann.lecun.com is often unavailable, and keeps them in data/.

From trail to input

A trail becomes an image the same way MNIST digits did, so the network sees what it was trained on:

  1. The trail's bounding box is scaled to fit 20×20 pixels, keeping its aspect ratio, and centred in a 28×28 image.
  2. It is drawn white on black with a px line (stroke_width in config.toml). The on-screen trail_width plays no part.
  3. The server moves the image's centre of mass to the middle.
  4. The 784 pixel values go through the network, and the label with the highest probability is the answer.

Train your own symbol

This page only reads drawings; it never trains. To teach the network a letter, a symbol or your own way of writing a digit, train it yourself from images:

  1. Make the images. One drawing per image, at least 30 per symbol, varying size, slant and position. Any size and any format Pillow reads (PNG, JPEG, BMP…). Draw a dark symbol on a plain light background, or a light one on black, with a pen about an eighth of the symbol's height thick, as in MNIST.
  2. Copy them into data/custom/<label>/. The folder's name is the label: data/custom/A/, data/custom/plus/. A digit's folder (data/custom/7/) adds your handwriting to that digit. File names don't matter.
  3. Run python3 train.py. It needs Pillow for this (pip install pillow). It crops each image to its symbol, scales it to fit 20×20 and centres it in 28×28, as MNIST was made. Labels that aren't digits become extra outputs after 0–9. Every 5th image of each label, in file-name order, is held out for testing; the rest are copied 1,000 times with small shifts, so a few dozen images aren't drowned out by MNIST's ~6,000 per digit.
  4. Restart server.py and reload the page. The new labels appear among the probabilities, and this tab shows how well they were learnt and which digits they get confused with.

A symbol unlike every digit (+, X) learns well from a few dozen images. One that resembles a digit (O and 0, l and 1, S and 5) gets confused with it, and the confusion matrix shows how often. For a whole alphabet, a letters dataset such as EMNIST Letters beats drawing hundreds of samples by hand. To go back to digits only, empty data/custom/ and train again.
